# Selection of the 5 Axes and 9 Agents

## 1. What is an "Axis"?

In our framework, an **axis** represents a fundamental design choice that every reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm must make. These design choices define how an algorithm learns, explores, and interacts with its environment. Different RL algorithms can be viewed as different combinations of these choices.

We identified **five key axes** that we believe have the greatest influence on how an agent may discover and exploit weaknesses in a reward function:

* **On-policy vs. Off-policy** – Whether the agent learns only from experiences generated by its current policy or can also reuse previously collected experiences.
* **Value-based vs. Policy-based vs. Actor-Critic** – Whether decisions are made using value estimates, a directly learned policy, or a combination of both.
* **Stochastic vs. Deterministic** – Whether the agent outputs a probability distribution over actions or selects a single deterministic action.
* **Exploration Strategy** – The mechanism used to discover new behaviors, such as entropy-based exploration, curiosity, novelty-seeking, or action noise.
* **Model-free vs. Model-based** – Whether the agent learns solely through real interactions with the environment or also builds an internal model to simulate future outcomes.

---

## 2. Why Were These Axes Chosen?

The objective of our project is not simply to evaluate the performance of RL algorithms, but to determine whether a **reward function itself is inherently exploitable**.

If our evaluation relied on only one family of algorithms—for example, actor-critic methods—and they all discovered the same exploit, it would be difficult to determine whether the exploit originated from the reward function or from biases shared by that class of algorithms.

By selecting agents that differ across these five fundamental axes, we ensure that they approach the environment using substantially different learning strategies. Consequently, if multiple, structurally different agents independently discover the same exploit, we gain stronger evidence that the vulnerability lies in the reward design rather than in the assumptions of any particular algorithm.

These five axes therefore serve as **experimental controls**, enabling us to evaluate reward functions from multiple perspectives and increasing the reliability of our conclusions.

## 4. Coverage of the Design Space

The selected agents collectively provide broad coverage across all five design axes.

* **On-policy vs. Off-policy:** PPO, PPO+ICM, and REINFORCE represent on-policy learning, while the remaining six agents are off-policy.
* **Learning Paradigms:** REINFORCE represents pure policy-based learning, NAF represents value-based learning, and the remaining agents employ actor-critic architectures.
* **Action Selection:** TD3, TD3+RND, and NAF produce deterministic actions, whereas the remaining agents learn stochastic policies.
* **Exploration Mechanisms:** The collection spans five distinct exploration strategies—entropy bonus, maximum entropy, Gaussian action noise, curiosity-driven exploration (ICM), and novelty-seeking through Random Network Distillation (RND).
* **Model-Free vs. Model-Based:** Seven agents are model-free, while MBPO and Dreamer represent two fundamentally different model-based approaches. MBPO uses short-horizon rollouts generated by a learned dynamics model, whereas Dreamer performs planning through latent-space world-model imagination.

---

## 5. Limitations

Two limitations of our agent selection should be acknowledged.

First, **ε-greedy exploration** and traditional discrete-action value-based algorithms do not have widely adopted counterparts in continuous control environments. Their absence reflects the characteristics of continuous-action reinforcement learning rather than an omission in our methodology.

Second, **MBPO** employs SAC as its underlying policy optimization algorithm. Therefore, MBPO should be interpreted as **SAC augmented with a learned dynamics model**, rather than as an entirely independent actor-critic algorithm. This design is intentional, as it isolates the impact of incorporating a world model while keeping the underlying policy-learning mechanism largely unchanged.
